16 October 2026 · App development update

A clearer way to learn.
More ways to remember.

A character should be the thing you notice first. We’ve been rebuilding the space around it: pictures to remember, components to explore, words to connect, and practice you can return to.

Inside the latest development build. These changes are implemented in the app; this is not an App Store or Google Play release announcement. The screens below come from a physical Samsung phone. Availability in the store build may differ.
RememberExplore wordsPractiseFeel at homeWhat’s ready

A picture when it helps. The shape when you need it.

Remember by picture now brings existing, credited artwork into character details, daily practice and lesson inspection. Switch between the picture and the character, enlarge the image, and open the source credits without leaving the learning flow.

The current bundle has meaning pictures for 131 of the 3,500 characters, plus exact-word picture matches for 226 dictionary headwords. Coverage is selective: a picture is shown only where we have a match. We have not generated thousands of illustrations or invented missing meanings.

There is another way to look closer, too. Component diagrams show how supported characters are arranged, with available meaning and sound hints. The source record can also be exported for inspection. They use existing character data; a component view is not a meaning picture, and an unknown source breakdown stays visibly unknown.

From one character to the words around it.

The new offline reference brings in 125,271 CC-CEDICT dictionary records, 32,028 Chinese–English sentence pairs from the Tatoeba-derived ManyThings collection, and HSK 2021 list membership. A further 29 HSK headwords have no dictionary match and remain explicitly incomplete.

Search words, readings and definitions, browse HSK levels, and open related words directly from a character. Word details retain alternative dictionary readings, traditional forms, available examples and source identities. Reference lookup stays separate from your lessons, learned-character collection and practice history.

These are imported source records, not model-written definitions or examples. HSK membership does not tell us which reading or sense is intended; the app keeps those alternatives and gaps visible. The imported definitions and example translations are Chinese–English, even when the app interface uses another language.

New characters today. Review when you choose.

Your daily goal now means new characters while unseen, eligible characters remain. You can adjust the goal from 1 to 20, subject to available content and access. Scheduled reviews no longer quietly take half of those new-character places. The day’s selected set survives an app restart, and the correction preserves existing learning history. When there are no unseen eligible characters left, daily practice can fall back to review.

Quick test lives inside You. Try recalling a learned character before revealing its pronunciation and meaning, then swipe left for “don’t know” or right for “know.” Buttons offer the same choices. The recap lets you revisit the characters you missed. These self-checks do not change your daily learning history, streak or XP.

You can also repeat today’s studied characters in another round. Premium access skips the ad gate. In the monetized Android development build, a free extra round requires three completed, optional rewarded ads; partial progress toward the unlock is saved. This ad-supported repeat option is not available on iOS yet, and the Android founding-tester distribution remains separate from monetized builds.

More room for the Chinese you came to learn.

Characters take the lead in the practice layout. Sentences read from left to right as connected text, with pronunciation and meaning nearby. Tap a character to inspect it; the surrounding sentence remains the context. The lesson library and You screen have clearer grouping, while Today is reachable from the main navigation.

The native work also adds system sharing, app shortcuts and reminder controls. Android includes a daily-character widget with streak information, an animated learning path, local XP and levels, answer-combo feedback, goal celebrations, a daily reward and streak sharing. A bundle of 18 short feedback sounds and haptic preferences let you choose how practice responds. Existing light, dark and system appearance choices remain available.

Stories continues the path from character to word, sentence and reading, with a saved place and optional pronunciation and meaning help. The two starter stories and lesson-based chapters build on the bundled lessons; expanded lessons and translations remain under language review.

What’s ready, and what comes next.

The latest full-access Android development build has been installed and checked on a physical Samsung, including the new-character queue, picture and component views, related words, Quick test and navigation. The current Android test suite passes 117 tests.

The iOS app and test targets compile, and the daily-queue core has been exercised separately. The latest interface, notifications and shortcuts still need a physical iPhone check; compiling the app does not establish that experience. We have not confirmed a new store release for this update.

The dated changelog records the individual changes and platform differences. The updated homepage shows more of the current Android interface. For the existing Android test, start with the joining guide.
